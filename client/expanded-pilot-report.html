Five-Pair LLM-LLM Capture Rate Analysis

This report combines the original pilot pair with the four expanded pilot pairs. Each pair played 10 baseline rounds and 10 signaling rounds in Phase 1. The primary dependent variables are per-pair stag capture rate and rabbit capture rate, defined as captures divided by 10 rounds. Error bars are +/- SEM across the 5 pairs.

Analysis Roadmap

The report first compares baseline and signaling outcomes, then uses baseline first actions to examine why rabbit hunting may occur when no explicit signal is available.

Primary Capture-Rate Comparison

Capture Rates

Finding. In baseline, LLM pairs captured rabbits in 27/50 rounds; in signaling, they captured rabbits in 0/50 rounds. This suggests that signaling can reduce the temptation to defect toward rabbits and may promote cooperation, even though cooperative stag capture remains difficult in this short-horizon grid, where each player has at most 10 moves before timeout.

Timeout Rate

Finding. Signaling reduced rabbit hunting but did not solve coordination. The signaling condition timed out in 47/50 rounds, compared with 15/50 rounds in baseline.

Baseline First-Action Analysis

To understand why baseline encourages rabbit hunting, we focus on baseline first actions. The key question is whether the players implicitly signal cooperative intent by choosing a temptation-free path. From Player 2's start, moving left is an implicit cooperative cue: it moves closer to the large target and farther from the nearest rabbit. Moving down is ambiguous because it moves closer to both the large target and the right-side rabbit.

Typical Baseline First Actions From The Initial Map

The initial map makes the asymmetry clear. Player 1 often outputs up, but the cell above Player 1 is blocked, so the action consumes a turn without moving. The log cannot tell whether this is deliberate waiting or an invalid-move tendency. Player 2 usually makes the first effective action, but most of those baseline first actions are ambiguous down moves rather than clear cooperative left moves.

Event-level first-action coding is available for the four expanded baseline pairs, 40 rounds. The original pilot baseline raw action history was not saved, so this explanatory first-action analysis uses 40 baseline rounds even though the capture-rate comparison above uses 50 baseline rounds.

Finding. In baseline, Player 1 usually did not move on the first action: 35/40 first actions were blocked no-moves. Player 2 usually made the first effective action, but most of these actions were ambiguous down moves: 27/35, compared with only 8/35 left moves that clearly signaled a temptation-free cooperative path.

Rabbit Captures By Player

As supporting detail for the baseline rabbit-hunting effect, rabbit captures were highly asymmetric: Player 1 captured 4 rabbits and Player 2 captured 23 rabbits across 50 baseline rounds. In signaling, neither player captured any rabbits across 50 rounds. The plots below show each pair separately; the x-axis is block number, with 2 trials per block. Lines show Player 1 and Player 2 rabbit capture counts within each block.

Finding. Baseline rabbit captures were concentrated in Player 2: 23 of the 27 baseline rabbit captures were by Player 2, consistent with Player 2's ambiguous first-action path toward the right-side rabbit.

Secondary Context

Signal Action Rate

Finding. Explicit signal actions were relatively rare even in the signaling condition. The behavioral difference is therefore not just frequent signaling; it also reflects how the available signal changes early action choices and discourages rabbit capture.
